# Design context — KiwiEvents

Persistent design guidance for this project. Read this before any design work.

## Design Context

### Users

The public surfaces (organizer profiles, event pages, checkout, ticket wallet)
are used by **a student on a phone who arrived from an Instagram story or a
WhatsApp link**. They are deciding in seconds, one-handed, probably in a group
chat, possibly on a bad connection. The job: *see what's on this weekend and
get a ticket without friction.*

That makes mobile the design, not a fallback. A layout that is beautiful at
1440px and merely survives at 390px has failed the actual user.

The admin surfaces (wp-admin) serve a different person: the organizer or venue
staff managing inventory, attendees and door scanning — a work tool, used on a
laptop, repeatedly.

### Brand personality

**Vibrant, social, celebratory.** Closer to a festival page than to a SaaS
platform. The interface should feel like something is happening tonight.

Crucially, that energy comes from **content, not effects**: big photography,
saturated accent color, typography with presence. It does *not* come from
glassmorphism, glow, or animation on everything — those were named explicitly
as anti-references and they also cost mobile performance.

### Aesthetic direction

Light. Photography-forward. Confident type. Generous but *uneven* spacing, so
the page has rhythm instead of uniform padding everywhere.

**Whose brand is it:** the structure, typography and layout are Campus Life's;
the **accent color belongs to the organizer**. This is not a preference, it is
the plugin's architecture — `admin/css/ke-admin-tokens.css` states it directly:
the Kiwi green is *the product's* brand and must never reach the public front
end, where each organizer's configured accent rules. Furia's blue is Furia's.

**Anti-references** — the design is wrong if it looks like any of these:
- a generic WordPress template: identical shadowed cards in a uniform grid,
  a rounded icon above every heading, everything centre-aligned;
- dark with neon glow, purple-to-blue gradients — the "an AI made this" look,
  and also where the leaked `#c36` purple came from;
- cold corporate: muted blue, seas of grey, no personality;
- over-effected: blur on every surface, animation on every element, huge
  shadows. Tiring to look at and slow on the phone that actually matters.

### Design principles

1. **Energy from content, not effects.** Let photos, accent color and type
   carry the feeling. Reach for blur, glow and motion only where they do a
   job — and remember every one of them is paid for on a mid-range phone.
2. **Mobile is the design.** Decide the 390px layout first, then let it grow.
   Never amputate functionality to make small screens fit.
3. **Every color resolves through a token, with a `var()` fallback.** The
   May 2026 colour audit exists because hardcoded hexes escaped the accent
   override and turned warm brands pink. A literal hex in a rule is a bug
   waiting for the next organizer who picks a different colour. Exception:
   email templates, where many clients strip custom properties.
4. **The soonest event is the page's job.** Whatever else is on screen, the
   next thing happening and its buy action come first and are reachable
   without thought.
5. **Nothing may read as a default.** No uniform card grid, no centre-
   everything, no stock rounded-icon headings. If it could be any WordPress
   plugin, it is wrong.
6. **Accessibility is part of "designed".** Visible focus for everything
   reachable by keyboard, real names on ARIA relationships, contrast that
   holds on a phone screen outdoors at night.

### Established system (discovered in code — keep using it)

- Public front end: `--kep-accent-1`, `--kep-accent-rgb`, `--kep-accent-grad`,
  `--kep-accent-glow`, `--kep-accent-soft`, `--kep-font`. Injected per site by
  `KE_Public::inject_color_vars()`.
- Plugin admin: `--kiwi-*` tokens in `admin/css/ke-admin-tokens.css`
  (Kiwi green, "Liquid Glass"). **Never enqueue on the front end.**
- Organizer profile: local `--op-*` tokens for neutrals, layered on top of the
  per-site accent.
- WP.com on campuslifepa.com injects `button { border:1px solid #c36; color:#c36 }`
  plus rose `:hover`/`:focus` variants. Any new button-bearing public surface
  needs the parent-scoped guard pattern used by `ke-promoter-portal.css`,
  `ke-tickets-wallet.css` and `ke-organizer-public.css`.
